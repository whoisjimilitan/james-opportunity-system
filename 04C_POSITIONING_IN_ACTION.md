# 04C — Positioning in Action: How James Becomes the Obvious Choice

Positioning is not a fixed identity. It's the result of understanding what the opportunity actually needs.

---

## The Core Principle

**Not:** "How do we make James sound good for this role?"

**But:** "Which version of James is the actual solution to what they need?"

Positioning emerges from discovery. You don't position James and then search for opportunities. You discover the opportunity and then realize which parts of James matter.

---

## Example: Training Coordinator vs. Regional Partner vs. Consultant

**Same person (James). Three different contexts. Three different positionings.**

### Context 1: A UK organization entering Ghana needs on-ground coordination

**What they need:**
- Someone in Ghana who understands their systems
- Someone who can solve problems in real-time
- Someone with credibility in the local context
- Someone who coordinates multiple parties

**Which James emerges:**
"Regional Partner and Implementation Lead"
- Emphasized: Regional access, coordination experience, institutional credibility
- De-emphasized: Specific crisis training background (not the point)
- Narrative: "You need someone on the ground who gets both the UK model and the Ghana context. That's rare. James has both."

### Context 2: A training platform needs someone to prepare facilitators

**What they need:**
- Someone who understands their content deeply
- Someone who can train others to deliver it
- Someone who knows what makes training land in different contexts
- Someone who's proven they can teach

**Which James emerges:**
"Training-of-Trainers Lead"
- Emphasized: Training delivery experience, understanding of how training lands differently across contexts, internal training record
- De-emphasized: Regional access (less relevant)
- Narrative: "Your success depends on facilitators who can deliver well. James trains facilitators to do that. He's done it internally. He knows what makes it work across different settings."

### Context 3: An organization needs advice on safeguarding systems

**What they need:**
- Someone who's implemented systems under pressure
- Someone who understands both best practice and local reality
- Someone with credibility from lived experience
- Advisor mentality, not just expertise

**Which James emerges:**
"Safeguarding Systems Advisor"
- Emphasized: Real implementation experience, trauma-informed approach, cross-cultural navigation
- De-emphasized: Training ability (not the point)
- Narrative: "You need someone who's actually implemented this in high-stakes environments and understands how systems have to adapt to work in your context. James brings both the rigor and the pragmatism."

---

## Why This Works

The positioning isn't invented. It's *discovered* from what the opportunity actually needs.

And it's **believable** because:
- It's based on what he's actually done
- It matches what they're actually looking for
- It's specific enough that another person wouldn't fit quite as well
- It's honest (doesn't exaggerate or invent)

---

## The Test

If you can't answer "which of these is true about James?" for a positioning, it's not real.

**Weak positioning:**
"James is a natural leader and a problem-solver who brings tremendous value."
(True-ish. Also true about 10,000 other people. Not positioning.)

**Strong positioning:**
"James has implemented crisis protocols in schools with 80+ vulnerable students under live pressure. He trains staff to do the same. That's what makes him credible for helping you scale your training into new markets."
(Specific. Verifiable. Explains why him and not someone else.)

---

## For the System

When discovery reveals an opportunity:
1. Ask: What does this actually need?
2. Ask: Which parts of James address that need?
3. Ask: Why would they choose James over another person with similar experience?
4. If you can answer #3 with specifics, you have positioning.
5. If you can't, go back to discovery.

Positioning that emerges from discovery is credible. Positioning that's invented for the pitch is noise.
