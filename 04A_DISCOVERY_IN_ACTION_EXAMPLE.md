# 04A — Discovery in Action: One Complete Example

This is not a template. This is a model of how intelligent discovery actually works.

Read it to understand what quality looks like, not to copy a structure.

---

## The Discovery: Safeguarding Platform Expansion Into West Africa

### The Signal (What Caught Attention)

A UK-based safeguarding training platform announced they were "exploring West African partnerships" to expand their content delivery. They'd received funding. No specific job posting, no RFP, no mention of James.

Initial hunch: They're expanding. That's real. But "exploring partnerships" is vague.

### The Research (What Changed Thinking)

**Question 1: Is this a real expansion signal, or just marketing language?**

Evidence gathered:
- They hired a "Regional Director - Africa" (real commitment, not exploration)
- They published a case study of a UK-Ghana partnership on mental health training (real work)
- Their annual report mentioned "scaling delivery into emerging markets" as strategic priority

Conclusion: Not marketing language. Actual expansion is happening.

**Question 2: What's the actual bottleneck?**

Investigated their training model:
- They create curriculum in UK
- They contract with local facilitators to deliver it
- Their materials are UK-contextualized (British examples, British institutional references)
- Success depends on local facilitators who understand both the content AND the local context

Bottleneck emerging: They can export curriculum, but delivery works better when local facilitators adapt it. Raw curriculum + unprepared local facilitator = training that doesn't land.

**Question 3: Who decides about West African expansion?**

Mapped the organization:
- CEO owns strategy
- Regional Director (newly hired) owns execution
- Partnerships team sources local facilitators
- No one person focuses on training quality in new markets

Key insight: The Regional Director is new, ambitious, and accountable for success. The partnerships team finds facilitators but doesn't validate their capability to deliver well.

**Question 4: What if we positioned this differently?**

Initial hypothesis: "James could be a facilitator in Ghana." 
Rejected: There are already facilitators in Ghana.

New hypothesis: "James could help them do what they actually need—ensure their content lands well in West African contexts when new facilitators deliver it."

Possible arrangements started forming:
- Quality advisor for West African content delivery (advise partnerships team)
- Training of trainers (prepare local facilitators before they deliver)
- Curriculum localization lead (adapt materials for West African context, not just translate)
- Regional implementation coordinator (oversee quality across multiple West African countries)

### The Counterfactual Test

**Original question: "What changes if James is involved?"**

Test each arrangement:

**If James is a quality advisor:**
- Without him: Regional Director hires local facilitators, some succeed, some fail. No pattern learning.
- With him: He identifies what makes good delivery in West African context, trains facilitators differently, scaling becomes more predictable.
- Change: From hit-or-miss to systematic. Worth their time? Probably. But is this credible? He'd need to understand their content AND West African realities. Check on James's profile: ✓ He has both.

**If James is training-of-trainers lead:**
- Without him: Facilitators get curriculum and are expected to deliver. Training quality varies wildly.
- With him: Facilitators get 3 days of training on delivery, context adaptation, audience management before going live. Success rate improves measurably.
- Change: From "we hope they can do this" to "we know they can because we trained them." Strong. Is this credible? He's trained staff internally. ✓ Probable.

**If James is curriculum localization lead:**
- Without him: UK curriculum is translated. British examples stay. References don't land.
- With him: Materials are adapted for West African context while preserving core content. Trainers can teach more naturally. Learners engage better.
- Change: From "we're serving UK content in West Africa" to "we're teaching in their language." Strong. Credible? He understands both contexts. ✓ Probable.

**If James is regional coordinator:**
- Without him: Regional Director tries to oversee Ghana + Nigeria + Senegal from UK. Quality is inconsistent.
- With him: He's in Ghana, understanding each market, coordinating across countries, ensuring quality standards, solving local problems.
- Change: From remote management (doesn't work) to on-ground coordination. Strong. Credible? He's based in Ghana, has regional knowledge, has coordination experience. ✓ Probable.

### Which Arrangement Is Strongest?

**Evidence check:**

Regional Director (new, accountable for success) would care most about:
1. **Scaling works** (not just expanding)
2. **Quality is consistent** (not hit-or-miss)
3. **They can show results to the CEO** (expansion succeeds, not fails)
4. **Local facilitators succeed** (their reputation depends on facilitator quality)

Which arrangement solves most of these?

- Quality advisor: Solves consistency, not scaling
- Training-of-trainers: Solves facilitator success, some consistency
- Localization lead: Solves engagement, some quality
- **Regional coordinator: Solves all of them.** He'd be their on-ground partner making the expansion actually work.

But: Regional coordinator implies bigger commitment, longer relationship, ongoing budget.

Smaller first step: Position him as **training-of-trainers lead** for the first market (Ghana).
- Low commitment: One country, defined timeline (6 months to train first cohort)
- Measurable: Did facilitators become better trainers?
- Proof of concept: If it works, expand to Nigeria, Senegal
- Path to bigger role: If successful, becomes Regional Coordinator

### The Counterfactual (Final Check)

**If James is removed from "training-of-trainers lead for Ghana":**
- Regional Director handles facilitator training himself (he's in UK, can't do this)
- Facilitators get training materials (not training)
- First cohort has inconsistent delivery (likely)
- CEO sees mixed results and pauses expansion (probable)

**If James is in the role:**
- Facilitators get real training in delivery
- First cohort succeeds
- Regional Director can show success to CEO
- Expansion accelerates

Clear counterfactual. His involvement matters.

### The Proposition (What's Ready)

**Discovery summary:**
- Signal: Real expansion happening (verified by hiring, case studies, strategy)
- Bottleneck: Quality delivery in new markets depends on facilitator preparation (research confirmed)
- James's role: Training-of-trainers lead for Ghana (credible based on his background, proven by counterfactual test)
- Their gain: First market succeeds, creates proof of concept for expansion
- James's gain: €15-25K (estimate based on 6-month engagement) + proof of capability + pathway to bigger role

**What's uncertain:**
- Whether they've identified this need explicitly (they might be assuming facilitators will self-prepare)
- Whether they have budget for training (they have expansion funding; this fits)
- Whether Regional Director has authority to commission this (he should; he's accountable for success)
- Whether James is interested in this kind of work (should be tested)

**What would invalidate it:**
- They already have a training program in place (would reveal this in conversation)
- They're not planning Ghana first (would change the arrangement)
- They want to keep training in-house (would kill the opportunity)
- They're moving slower than we think (would change timing)

**Smallest next step to test:**
Brief conversation with Regional Director:
"I noticed your West African expansion. Your success depends on facilitators delivering well in new contexts. We've seen organizations stumble here. Would it be worth 30 minutes to talk about how Ghana's first cohort of facilitators gets prepared?"

Low commitment. Clear problem. Specific ask.

## Why This Discovery Is Strong

1. **Signal is verified** — Not "they might expand," but "they're actively expanding now"
2. **Need is researched** — Not assumed, but evidenced through their structure and hiring
3. **James's role is specific and credible** — Not "he could help," but "here's exactly what he'd do and why he's qualified"
4. **Counterfactual is clear** — Expansion works better with him than without him
5. **Arrangement is realistic** — Fits their budget, timeline, and structure
6. **The ask is proportionate** — Conversation, not commitment
7. **Revenue is credible** — Based on market research, not speculation
8. **Uncertainty is explicit** — Known unknowns, not hidden assumptions

### Why This Discovery Is NOT a Template

- It's not "follow these steps"
- It shows how thinking actually evolved (rejected hypotheses, changed directions)
- It shows the mess (many arrangements considered, then narrowed)
- It shows the judgment (choosing training-of-trainers as first step, not regional coordinator)
- It shows what strong looks like (evidence, counterfactual, credibility)
- Each discovery will have different logic, different arrangement, different path

The point: Learn to recognize this quality of thinking. Not the structure, but the rigor.

---

## For Claude Code

Use this as a model for what "done" looks like for a discovery:
- Signal: verified, current, real
- Bottleneck: researched and evidenced
- Arrangement: multiple options explored, best one selected with reasoning
- Counterfactual: tested and clear
- Revenue: credible with estimation method shown
- Uncertainty: explicit
- Next step: specific and proportionate

This is what audacious, grounded discovery looks like.
